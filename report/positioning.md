## (a) Verified reference list

### Interpretability-based collusion detection

**R1. Rose, A., Cullen, C., Abdelnabi, S., Torr, P., Kaplowitz, B. G. and Schroeder de Witt, C. (2026). Detecting Multi-Agent Collusion Through Multi-Agent Interpretability. arXiv:2604.01151.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01151
- Version history: v1 submitted 1 April 2026; current v4 dated 12 October 2026 (the abs page lists v4; no revision dated 15 October was found). Also a poster at the NeurIPS 2026 Trustworthy AI for Good (AI4GOOD) workshop (https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/165391), and two LessWrong posts dated 2 and 3 April 2026 (https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/sESEv3HT4qvAL7Hdk/..., https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Q65SETuTmhF4LWdix/...).
- The name **HELIOS** (expanded in the current PDF as a hidden-state ensemble lens for identification of scheming) appears in the v4 PDF only, not in v1, the abstract page, the NeurIPS listing or the LessWrong posts. Cite HELIOS as the framework name in v4 of 2604.01151, not as a separate paper. **NARCBench** is the benchmark: Core (50 committee-deliberation scenarios, 17 domains, leave-domain-out), Transfer (six structurally different scenario types, zero-shot), Stego (steganographic blackjack card counting, zero-shot); v4 adds a Verdict tier for emergent, uninstructed collusion.
- Internals: logistic-regression "deception direction" on last-token residual-stream activations (v1: layers 26 to 30 of 64 on Qwen3-32B, 4-bit). Five group-level aggregators over per-agent scores: Peak Suspicion, Deception Split, Asymmetry Probe, Activation Alignment (mean pairwise cosine of raw activations, the only one without the probe), Sorted Concatenation. No attention or SAE features.
- Models: v1 Qwen3-32B only. v4 abstract says four open-weight models and six probe architectures, strongest Llama-3.1-70B. One fetch of the abs page listed the four as Qwen3-32B, Llama-3.1-70B, DeepSeek-R1 32B, GPT-OSS-20B; the PDF text I could read names only Qwen3, Llama-3.1-70B and gpt-oss, so treat the full list as PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Headline numbers (v4): 1.00 AUROC in-distribution on every model; 0.73 to 0.93 AUROC zero-shot on Transfer with Llama-3.1-70B; 0.99 to 1.00 on Stego; recall at 1% FPR only 0.38 on Transfer; Verdict (emergent) best 0.73 AUROC. An LLM transcript judge is at chance on Stego.
- Controls: null probe trained on a random split of honest agents (about 0.49 AUROC); collusion-free control runs; LLM judges; v1 mentions aggressive-persona honest agents.
- Stated limitations (Appendix A, v4): probes may rely on spurious correlations; prompt confounds mitigated, not eliminated; labels reflect instruction, not confirmed execution; adversarial robustness and evaluation awareness largely untested; fixed 4-agent, 2-colluder group; no sequential stopping guarantees; GLRT assumptions do not generally hold; AUROC hides performance at deployment false-positive rates.
- Directly relevant to the J-lens half: **v1 explicitly says a privileged-observer control (an agent that knows the scheme but is told not to take part) would separate knowing about collusion from taking part, and leaves it to future work.** The v4 text I read does not mention it being run. So "comprehension without adoption" has been named as a gap by the authors themselves, not closed.

**R2. Li, D., Varigonda, A., Feng, A., Chauhan, M. and Shrivastava, D. (2026). Collusion Does Not Localize to Jacobian-Lens Transport Subspaces. Poster, NeurIPS 2026 Third Workshop on Agents in the Wild: Safety, Security, and Beyond.** https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/168189 (no arXiv id found; only the poster listing was read).
- Tests whether a collusion probe restricted to the J-lens transport subspace beats a capacity-matched random subspace under a permutation null. Across three model families (unnamed on the listing), four multi-agent environments and eight depths, it does not. The same procedure does detect an agent's stated stance. Their one-line summary is that the subspace finds what agents say, not what they withhold.
- Stated limitation: no single comparison survives correction over 72 tests; the claim rests on consistent ordering. THIS IS THE CLOSEST PRIOR WORK TO THE J-LENS HALF. It does not run a refusal or comprehension-without-adoption control.

**R3. Veras Neves, C. E. (2026). j-space: Jacobian Lens for algorithmic collusion. Hugging Face static Space (Trackio logbook), created 26 July 2026, runs 13 to 25 July 2026.** https://hf.co/spaces/carlosveras/jspace-jacobian-lens-collusion
- Models: Qwen3.5-4B and gemma-4-e4b-it (not gemma-4-12B-it). Single prompts, greedy decoding, seed 0, single forward passes; no sampling, no multi-agent game.
- Five "claims": (1) the J-lens reads a Yes/No margin shift and a rank collapse of "independent" in a mid-depth band (Qwen L10 to L29, Gemma L14 to L39) that the logit lens does not show; (2) controls `no_echo` (coordination described without a trigger word) and `open_concept` (no Yes/No slot) reduce but do not rule out lexical-echo and answer-slot explanations; (3) read/write asymmetry: steering along the first-order J-transpose direction never flips the verdict (0 of 36 cells on Qwen) while activation patching of the last-position residual flips it at L22; (4) dose-response: band-mean margin orders explicit collusion > inferred coordination > an innocuous-contact negative control (weather chat plus equal prices); (5) the cross-model difference is mostly a baseline-margin effect.
- Conditions are `independent`, `collusion`, `no_echo`, `neg_control`, `open_concept`. **There is no condition in which the model comprehends a coded proposal and refuses it, and no matched adopt-versus-refuse pair.** The author explicitly says the lens reads a scenario type, not an intent. The LinkedIn post referred to in the task was NOT FOUND by search; treat its content as UNVERIFIED and cite only the Space.

### Workspace and lens methods

**R4. Gurnee, W., Sofroniew, N., Pearce, A., Piotrowski, M., Kauvar, I., Chen, R., Soligo, A., Bogdan, P., Ong, E., Wang, R., Thompson, T. B., Abrahams, D., Kantamneni, S., Ameisen, E., Batson, J. and Lindsey, J. (2026). Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models. Transformer Circuits Thread, 6 July 2026.** https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html
- Introduces the Jacobian lens (layer-averaged Jacobian to the final layer, then unembed) and the J-space. Main analyses on Claude Sonnet 4.5, with Haiku 4.5, Opus 4.5 and Opus 4.6; open models appear only as Neuronpedia-hosted lenses.
- Two findings relevant to "represented while suppressed": (i) under an instruction to ignore a concept, the concept still appears in the lens, at a lower rate than under a focus instruction (they compare this to the white bear effect), and an appendix reports that earlier-layer J-lens vectors are needed to actively suppress mention of a concept; (ii) in the selectivity experiments a concept (the language name) is present in the lens in every task but is causally used only in some, so presence in the workspace does not imply the model acts on it. Stated limitation: the J-space never exceeds about 10% of excess variance and the authors do not claim all strategic cognition routes through it.

**R5. Ayonrinde, K. and Lindsey, J. (2026). J++ Lens: Jacobian Filtering Enables More Faithful Workspace Lenses. LessWrong, 8 October 2026.** https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nc9dHfcB22JdMzGbr/j-lens-jacobian-filtering-enables-more-faithful-workspace-lenses (no arXiv id).
- Method: k-means clustered expert Jacobians with learned weights, LRP backward pass, readout filtering. Recall@10 on latent-variable extraction 55.2% vs 35.7% for J-lens on Qwen3.6-27B; evaluated on six models (9B to 284B) including Gemma 4 31B (58.0%).
- Retrieve-then-suppress: the authors hypothesise a two-step mechanism in which an instruction about a concept (focus or avoid) first brings it into the workspace in early layers, and later layers amplify or de-amplify it. Evidence in some but not all cases. Concepts often remain in the workspace when the model is told to avoid them, and ablating the early representation made the model worse at avoiding the concept. This is the cleanest published statement that a concept can be held while being suppressed, and it is what the J-lens half of the report should cite for that premise.

### Action-only and information-theoretic detection

**R6. Bonjour, T., Aggarwal, V. and Bhargava, B. (2022). Information theoretic approach to detect collusion in multi-agent games. UAI 2022, PMLR 180:223-232.** Already in the report; not re-verified here.

**R7. Xu, X., Wu, C., Lu, J., Tan, K., Tao, S. and Hong, H. (2026). Collusion with Competitive Marginals: Price-Level Audits Are Blind by Construction. arXiv:2607.26385 [cs.GT], 29 July 2026.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26385
- Two deployments of one base model show small residual bid correlation (+0.053 at temperature 0.3 under the strictest auditor, cross-base about 0.0001), falling with temperature; honest Ethereum block-builder pairs are already strongly dependent. Statistics: residual pairwise correlation with permutation-null family-wise thresholds, Fisher-z with Bonferroni, swap regret. No conditional MI or net influence. Their policy conclusion is that conduct detection is blind by construction and regulators should count independent decision-makers. The report's current citation ("Xu, et al.") should be completed with this title and author list.

**R8. Tailor, O. (2025). Audit the Whisper: Detecting Steganographic Collusion in Multi-Agent LLMs. arXiv:2510.04303.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04303
- Cross-run mutual information (Donsker-Varadhan and NWJ critics, plus a discretised plug-in), calibrated on 200 honest seeds per task and 10,000 honest audits, with Holm and Westfall-Young corrections. No conditional MI, transfer entropy or directed information. Operates on transcripts, not actions. Single author; internal inconsistencies between claimed and tabulated TPRs.

**R9. Ghanem, M. C. (2026). Steganalysis of Adaptive Covert Collusion in Tool-Using Agent Populations: A Black-Box, Cross-Principal Approach. arXiv:2608.02698 [cs.CR], 3 August 2026.** Cross-run MI with an identity-shuffled null plus token-statistic, timing and tool-call features. Results tables are labelled illustrative placeholders; no measured detection results. Cite only as a proposal.

### Concurrent Apart sprint entries (23 to 25 October 2026), from https://apartresearch.com/sprints/ai-collusion-research-sprint-2026-10-23-to-2026-10-25

These are concurrent, not prior, work, and the sprint page states they are early-stage participant work. They matter for the novelty claim because judges will see them side by side.

**S1. Ayeku, D. Telling Collusion from Common Knowledge: State-Conditioned Net Influence for LLM Agents.** https://apartresearch.com/sprints/projects/telling-collusion-from-common-knowledge-stateconditioned-net-influence-for-llm-agents-p0im
- Net influence on four Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct agents in 60-round four-player Rock-Paper-Scissors, 100 games, five conditions. Shows the fixed threshold gives 12 to 32% false alarms at 60 rounds, adds a per-game permutation test, conditions on a coarse public state, and includes two innocent agents following the same public rule (83% of rounds) as a parallel-conduct confound. Correlation flags the innocent pair in 19 of 20 games; conditioned net influence flags 2 of 20 and never the innocent pair. Reports that conditioning costs power (14 of 20 vs 20 of 20). Baselines: calibrated correlation and an LLM judge. **This entry independently covers all three of the report's contributions (implementation on LLM agents, permutation calibration, state-conditioned parallel-conduct confound).**

**S2. Snow, J. Reading Moves, Not Messages: Detecting Collusion Between LLM Agents.** https://apartresearch.com/sprints/projects/reading-moves-not-messages-detecting-collusion-between-llm-agents-9mgg

## (b) Related work (rewrite, 343 words)

**Information-theoretic detection from actions.** Bonjour, Aggarwal and Bhargava (2022) define influence between agents as conditional mutual information given the game state and flag a pair whose net influence exceeds a fixed threshold. The method was validated on three scripted or Q-learning players with no baseline and no public code, and the threshold's behaviour at short horizons was not examined. Later information-theoretic detectors for language-model agents work on transcripts rather than actions: Tailor (2025) and Ghanem (2026) estimate cross-run mutual information over messages with honest-replay calibration, and neither conditions on a public state. We found no application of transfer entropy or directed information to LLM coordination. Xu et al. (2026) show that two deployments of one base model carry a small residual bid correlation and conclude that price-level audits are blind by construction; this is the LLM form of the parallel-conduct confound we test. Within the same sprint, Ayeku (2026) and Snow (2026) independently apply net influence with a permutation test to LLM players, and Ayeku also conditions on a public state against an innocent rule-following pair. Our scripted sweep (4,400 games, five horizons, six collusion strengths, four baselines, two state functions, with and without correction) is the systematic ground-truth evaluation that those concurrent LLM-first entries do not report.

**Detection from internals.** Rose et al. (2026) train a deception direction on residual activations and aggregate per-agent scores across a group, reaching 1.00 AUROC in-distribution and 0.73 to 0.93 zero-shot. They name, and leave to future work, a privileged-observer control in which an agent knows the scheme but does not take part. Li et al. (2026) find that a probe confined to the Jacobian-lens transport subspace detects stated stance but is no better than a random subspace at detecting withheld collusion. Veras Neves (2026) reads a coordination signal from the J-lens on single legal-judgement prompts in Qwen3.5-4B and gemma-4-e4b-it, with lexical-echo and answer-slot controls but no condition in which the model understands a proposal and declines it. Gurnee et al. (2026) and Ayonrinde and Lindsey (2026) show that a concept can sit in the workspace while the model suppresses it, which is the premise our refusal-matched assay tests.

**Evidence standards.** Berger et al. (2025) survey plus factors beyond parallelism. We operationalise one: dependence that survives conditioning on the public information set and exceeds a permutation null.

## (c) Novelty statement (121 words)

What is new here is narrow. We release the first general-N implementation of Bonjour et al.'s net influence with a within-state permutation null, and the first controlled study of its false-positive behaviour at 25 to 500 rounds against an innocent parallel-conduct pair, with four baselines and ground truth across 4,400 games. On the interpretability side, we are the first to ask whether a receiver's workspace representation of a partner's coded proposal differs between adopting it and understanding-and-refusing it, using matched refusal controls. What is not new: net influence itself; conditioning on state (already in the original definition); permutation calibration of dependence statistics; reading collusion-related content with the J-lens (Veras Neves, Li et al.); activation probes for collusion (Rose et al.); and the observation that same-model agents correlate (Xu et al.).

## (d) Risk: where prior or concurrent work already does what the report claims

1. The abstract's claims that "no public implementation exists" and that net influence "has never been applied to language-model agents" are contradicted by two concurrent sprint entries: Ayeku's "Telling Collusion from Common Knowledge" implements state-conditioned net influence with a per-game permutation test on Qwen2.5-7B agents and tests it against an innocent public-rule pair, and Snow's "Reading Moves, Not Messages" applies an action-only permutation-tested detector to Gemma 4 agents and auction rings; soften to "no public implementation existed before this sprint" and position the scripted ground-truth sweep, the fixed-alpha bias analysis and the four-baseline comparison as the distinct contribution.

2. Li et al. (NeurIPS 2026 workshop) already report, with a permutation null and a capacity-matched random-subspace control, that the J-lens subspace tracks what agents say rather than what they withhold, so a J-lens assay that finds no adopt-versus-refuse separation would be a replication of their negative result, and one that does find separation must be reconciled with it.

3. Rose et al. v1 explicitly name the knows-but-does-not-participate control as the way to separate comprehension from participation, and the "Who's the cartel" sprint entry shows an activation probe's verdict is determined by which negatives it was trained against, so the report should present its refusal-matched control as executing a test others have proposed rather than as identifying a gap nobody had noticed.
